The problem

Recognition happened. Nobody could see it.

People did remarkable things for each other every quarter — but that work stayed in inboxes and hallway conversations. And when it was time to choose Employee of the Quarter and of the Year, HR had to manually facilitate the leadership team: collecting names over email, chasing votes, and reconciling opinions with no shared record of who was nominated, why, or who supported them.

No visibility

Good work went unseen. Recognition depended on who happened to notice — and who spoke up in the room.

No shared story

Nominations were a name and a sentence. There was no compelling story to rally the leadership team behind.

No fair process

HR chased managers one by one. Selection was opaque — no vote counts, no record of who backed whom.

No audit trail

"How was this decided?" had no clean answer. The whole thing rested on spreadsheets and memory.

How it works

Four steps, one open process

Scroll to walk through the flow — from a written story to an announced winner.

01 Anyone can nominate

Every employee nominates — by telling a story

Recognition isn't left to managers. Anyone on the team can nominate a colleague by writing the story of what they did and why it mattered. A real story is what moves a leadership team — and it makes recognition personal, not procedural.

02 Managers vote

Managers vote — within and across functions

Every story goes to all managers company-wide. They vote for their own people and for standout stories in other teams too — so a great story from Finance is backed by leaders in Sales, Tech and Operations. Votes are tallied live.

03 Heads select by quota

Function heads pick the winners — in the open

Each function head sees only their own people's stories, the vote counts, and exactly who voted for whom. They select the final winners within a set quota — a decision made on evidence everyone can see, not a closed-door debate.

04 Recognition, visible

Winners announced, stories celebrated

The shortlist assembles automatically and the stories are shared. Recognition becomes something the whole company can see — and every step is captured in a clean audit trail.
